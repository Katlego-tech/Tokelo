Where a lease is a fixed-term consumer agreement under the Consumer Protection Act, section 14 says the consumer may cancel it at any time by giving the supplier 20 business days' notice in writing or in another recorded form, whatever the agreement says. On cancelling, the consumer still owes whatever was due under the agreement up to that date. The supplier may charge a reasonable cancellation penalty, and must credit the consumer with anything that remains the consumer's (section 14(2)(b) and 14(3)). The section does not apply to agreements between juristic persons (section 14(1)).

Between 40 and 80 business days before a fixed term ends, the supplier must tell the consumer in writing of the coming end date, of any material changes if it continues, and of the consumer's options. Unless the consumer asks for it to end or agrees to a new fixed term, it then continues month to month (section 14(2)(c) and 14(2)(d)).

Under the Rental Housing Act, if a lease expires and the tenant stays on with the landlord's consent, without a new written lease, it becomes a periodic lease on the same terms, and either side must give at least one month's written notice to end it (section 5(5)). A tenant who leaves before the lease ends, without notice, is taken to have ended it on the date the landlord established they had left, but the landlord keeps its rights arising from the breach (section 5(3)(o)).
