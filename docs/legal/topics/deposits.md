Under the Rental Housing Act, a landlord may ask for a deposit before the tenant moves in. The landlord must invest it in an interest-bearing account with a financial institution, at a rate no lower than that institution's savings rate, and pay the interest to the tenant. During the lease the tenant may ask for written proof of the interest earned, and the landlord must give it. Different rules apply where the landlord is a registered estate agent (section 5(3)(c) and 5(3)(d)).

When the lease ends:
- If nothing is owed, the whole deposit and its interest must be refunded, without any deduction, within 7 days (section 5(3)(i)).
- If something is owed, the landlord may apply the deposit and interest to amounts the tenant owes under the lease, including the reasonable cost of repairing damage and replacing lost keys. The rest must be refunded within 14 days of the dwelling being handed back, and the receipts for those costs must be open to the tenant (section 5(3)(g) and 5(3)(h)).
- If the landlord did not inspect the dwelling with the tenant at the start or the end, that is taken as the landlord accepting it is in a good state of repair, and the full deposit plus interest must be refunded (section 5(3)(j)).
- If the tenant did not respond to the request for a joint inspection at the end, the landlord inspects within 7 days, and must refund any balance within 21 days (section 5(3)(k) to 5(3)(m)).

These terms are part of every lease, and neither side can waive them (section 5(4)).
