Before a tenant moves in, the Rental Housing Act says the tenant and the landlord must inspect the dwelling together for defects or damage, with a view to deciding what the landlord is responsible for putting right, or to recording it. The defects found are listed in an annexure to the lease (section 5(3)(e) and 5(7)).

A landlord may enter the dwelling to make repairs, but only on reasonable notice, and at reasonable times (Gauteng's Unfair Practices Regulations, regulation 10).

Lack of maintenance is one of the unfair practices the Rental Housing Tribunal may rule to stop, and any tenant may lodge a complaint about an unfair practice with the Tribunal. From the day a complaint is lodged until the Tribunal rules, or three months pass, whichever comes first, the landlord must effect necessary maintenance, and the tenant must keep paying the rent (section 13(1), 13(4) and 13(7)).

When a lease ends, the landlord is entitled to get the dwelling back in a good state of repair, fair wear and tear excepted (section 4(5)(d)).
