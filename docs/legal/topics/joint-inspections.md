The Rental Housing Act makes two joint inspections part of every lease:
- Before the tenant moves in, the tenant and the landlord must inspect the dwelling together for defects or damage, with a view to deciding what the landlord is responsible for putting right, or to recording it. The list of defects is attached to the lease (section 5(3)(e) and 5(7)).
- When the lease ends, the landlord and the tenant must arrange a joint inspection at a mutually convenient time, within the three days before the lease expires, to see whether any damage was caused while the tenant lived there (section 5(3)(f)).

If the landlord fails to inspect with the tenant present, at the start or at the end, that is taken as the landlord accepting the dwelling is in a good and proper state of repair. The landlord then has no further claim against the tenant, who must be refunded the full deposit plus interest (section 5(3)(j)).

If the tenant does not respond to the landlord's request for the end-of-lease inspection, the landlord must inspect within seven days after the lease ends, and may deduct the reasonable cost of repairing damage and replacing lost keys from the deposit (section 5(3)(k) and 5(3)(l)).

The landlord may enter on reasonable notice for these inspections, or once either side has said it intends to end the lease (Gauteng's Unfair Practices Regulations, regulation 10).
