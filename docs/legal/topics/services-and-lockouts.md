Gauteng's Unfair Practices Regulations say a landlord who must supply water, electricity or gas, under the law or the lease, must provide it. The landlord may not cause the supply to be cut or interrupted without a court order, except in an emergency, or after reasonable notice to do maintenance, repairs or renovations, and then it must be restored as soon as reasonably possible. If the tenant has paid the landlord for services, the landlord may not put them at risk by failing to pay the supplier (regulation 13(1)).

A landlord may not lock a tenant out, or have one locked out, without a court order. Neither side may change the locks or doors to the dwelling unless it is needed because of wear and tear or another reasonable cause, after reasonable notice to the other, and with duplicate keys given to the other at once (regulation 9(3) and 9(4)).

Someone who occupies without the owner's consent or any other right in law, whom the PIE Act calls an unlawful occupier, may not be evicted except on the authority of a court order, and evicting them without one is an offence (sections 1 and 8).

A tenant may lodge a complaint about an unfair practice with the Rental Housing Tribunal, which may issue spoliation orders and grant interdicts (Rental Housing Act, section 13(1) and 13(12)).
